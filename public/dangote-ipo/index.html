What is an IPO?

Simple Definition

An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is when a private company offers its shares to the public for the first time on a stock exchange.

This allows ordinary people to buy ownership stakes in the company — becoming shareholders.

05

How Does an IPO Work?

1

Company Decides to Go Public

The company decides to raise money by selling shares to the public instead of just private investors.

2

Regulators Approve the Offer

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) reviews the company's documents and approves the IPO.

3

Shares Are Offered to the Public

The company sets a price and a number of shares. Investors apply to buy during the offer period.

4

Shares Start Trading

After the offer closes, shares are listed on the stock exchange. Investors can then buy and sell freely.

Why Do Companies Do IPOs?

To Raise Money

Companies need capital to expand, build new factories, pay off debt, or fund new projects. An IPO raises large amounts of money quickly.

To Grow the Business

With public funding, companies can invest in new products, hire more workers, and expand into new markets.

Why It Matters to You

IPOs give ordinary Nigerians the chance to own shares in big companies. But they also carry risks — understanding how they work helps you make informed decisions.

Remember

Not every IPO is a good investment. Some succeed, some fail. Always do your own research before investing.

How to Invest — Steps 1 to 3

1

Open a CSCS Account

A Central Securities Clearing System (CSCS) account is required to hold shares in Nigeria. You can open one through a licensed stockbroker or a bank that offers stockbroking services.

2

Choose a Licensed Stockbroker

Only buy shares through a stockbroker registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the NGX. Visit sec.gov.ng to verify licensed operators.

3

Apply During the Offer Period

For the Dangote IPO: apply between September 14 and October 13, 2026 through your broker or approved online platforms. Minimum: 10 shares (₦5,250).

How to Invest — Steps 4 & 5

4

Pay in Full on Application

Payment is due in full when you apply — not in installments. Make sure you have the correct amount in your account before applying.

5

Wait for Allotment & Listing

After the offer closes, shares are allocated to investors. Once listed on the NGX, you can buy or sell shares through your broker at market prices.

Important: Never buy shares through unofficial channels, social media links, or people who approach you directly. Only use SEC-licensed operators.

Investment Risks — General

HIGH RISK

Loss of Money

You can lose some or all of the money you invest. Stock prices can fall below what you paid — there is no guarantee of getting your money back.

HIGH RISK

Price Fluctuation

Share prices change daily based on demand and supply. There is no guarantee shares will increase in value — they can go down as well as up.

MEDIUM RISK

No Guaranteed Returns

Dividends are not guaranteed. Companies may reduce or stop paying dividends depending on their performance. You may receive nothing.

Investment Risks — IPO Specific

MEDIUM RISK

No Trading History

IPOs have no public price history. It's harder to predict how the share price will perform after listing compared to established companies.

MEDIUM RISK

Hype & Overexcitement

IPOs often generate excitement. Be careful not to invest just because others are doing it — do your own research first.

GOOD TO KNOW

Lock-Up Periods

Existing shareholders may be restricted from selling for a period after listing, which can affect share supply and price.

How to Avoid Scams — Red Flags

SCAM WARNING

"Guaranteed Returns"

No legitimate investment guarantees profits. If someone promises guaranteed returns, it is almost certainly a scam.

SCAM WARNING

"Double Your Money Quickly"

Any scheme promising to double or triple your money fast is fraudulent. Real investments take time and carry risk.

SCAM WARNING

Pressure to Act Fast

Scammers create urgency: "Invest now or miss out!" Legitimate investments give you time to think and research.

SCAM WARNING

Unlicensed Operators

Anyone not registered with SEC or NGX is operating illegally. Always verify before investing. Check sec.gov.ng.

How to Protect Yourself

Verify the Company & Broker

Check SEC's website (sec.gov.ng) to confirm the company and stockbroker are licensed before investing.

Use Official Channels Only

Apply through licensed stockbrokers or the company's official website — never through WhatsApp groups, social media links, or unknown people.

Never Share Your PIN or Password

No legitimate broker will ask for your CSCS PIN, bank PIN, or BVN password. Keep these secret at all times.

Get Everything in Writing

Insist on official receipts, contract notes, and documentation. Keep records of all transactions.

Report Suspicious Activity

If you suspect fraud, report to SEC (sec.gov.ng) or the Nigeria Police. Help protect others from scams.

Key Takeaways

1

An IPO is Not a Guaranteed Win

Just because a company is big doesn't mean its shares will always go up. All investments carry risk.

2

Do Your Own Research

Read the official prospectus. Understand the company. Don't invest based on hype or rumors.

3

Only Use Licensed Channels

Buy shares only through SEC-licensed stockbrokers. Never send money to individuals or unverified platforms.

4

Invest Only What You Can Afford to Lose

Never invest money you need for rent, food, school fees, or emergencies. The stock market is not a savings account.

5

When in Doubt, Ask a Professional

Consult a licensed financial advisor or stockbroker before making any investment decision.
